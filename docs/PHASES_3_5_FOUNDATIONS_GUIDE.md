# Parkbound graphics, large sandbox and coaster test guide (0.75.0)

From **New Park**, choose Sandbox and click **Map** to cycle 32, 64, 128 and 256 tiles per side. The 32-tile choice retains the familiar flat starter paths, guests and coaster. Larger maps start empty with flat heights, $1,000 cash and the existing lake/shore materials. Click **Landscape** to cycle Flat or rolling hills from seeds 1–3. Challenge parks remain the fixed 32-tile scenario layout. Press Enter on New Park to create the selected park; the Create action remains the initial keyboard focus.

Larger maps start with a central 32×32 owned area and a one-tile owned corridor to the west edge. Owned and unowned terrain have different colors and the ownership boundary is marked. In **Build**, choose **Buy Land / $5**, then click an unowned tile touching your land. Buying is immediate, paid, and saved. Build tools reject unowned land with a reason. Lay a public path from the west edge, set a visitor entry on that path, and enable arrivals. Build access, queues, facilities and rides as usual. The camera allows proportionally closer zoom and wider panning on larger maps; **R** restores the whole-map view.

The **Raise**, **Lower**, **Water**, **Grass** and **Sand** tools in **Build** use a square brush. **Brush +/−** changes the radius from 0 to 2 (1×1, 3×3 or 5×5). Hover shows the affected area and the exact price before clicking. Raising or lowering costs $1 per changed tile, material painting $0.50, and water $2. The whole brush is rejected without payment if any tile is unowned, blocked, at an invalid height or unaffordable. Water needs level ground. Ground paths and objects block flooding; a bridge at height 2 or more can remain over water. New ground paths on water are rejected. The **T** terrain preset shortcut is limited to a fresh, empty, fully owned park; choose the landscape in New Park for large maps.

For coaster engineering, close and empty the installed coaster, open **Rides [Y] → Test**, and run one isolated lap. The report lists the three fastest track pieces by piece number, kind, tile and speed. It uses the same motion rules as the live train but is a derived estimate. Changing the track or operating settings invalidates the old result. The test leaves guests, the park clock, finances and save bytes unchanged. The Track editor now includes **Bank Left**, **Bank Right** and **Launch** pieces. Banked turns allow higher speed than ordinary curves; launch pieces accelerate the train. They follow the existing connection, collision, quote, payment and save rules.

Guest clothing now has 48 deterministic appearances, including occasional caps. This art is generated from guest IDs and adds no save fields. The [visual bible](VISUAL_BIBLE.md) and bundled `assets/manifest.json` record the original art rules and shipped asset families.

The full Phase 3–5 roadmap gates remain open. The remaining work includes consistent undo and refunds for landscaping, larger path/object/guest capacities, native display and rotation checks, more original ride art and night/weather, inversions and special track, multiple independent coasters and trains, block dispatch, breakdown/repair and the complete custom-build-to-export acceptance flow. The 256-tile choice is supported by save and edit tests, but this development PC is not a performance benchmark.
