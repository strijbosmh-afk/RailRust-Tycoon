# Parkbound graphics, large sandbox and coaster test guide (0.82.0)

In **Menu → Settings**, choose **Lighting** to cycle Day, Sunset and Night, and **Weather** to switch Clear/Rain presentation. Lamps illuminate public walkways at night. Pausing freezes decorative motion. These choices are saved with display preferences and can be changed while a park is open.

Connected paths entering a fully buried tunnel now show a masonry entrance in Surface view. Underground view exposes the complete route for editing. Portals follow the connecting path height and disappear when the outside connection is removed.

**Path and queue prices:** New paths placed with Build cost $1 flat or $1.50 for a one-step slope. Queue pieces cost $2 flat or $2.50 sloped. The bottom status bar keeps the selected path/queue price visible, and hover shows the exact build price; unaffordable placement is rejected before allocating an object. Demolition refunds half the path price plus any separately paid furniture refund. Older, already placed paths and fixture paths have no path-price basis and give no path refund. The finance ledger and recorded park value include the paid pieces. Schema/content 44/44 saves retain that basis; older parks migrate with free existing paths.

**Undo paused edits:** Pause the game, then use **Undo Ctrl+Z** and **Redo Ctrl+Y** in the left sidebar. These controls reverse up to 16 recent park changes, restoring terrain, objects, guests, cash and finance entries together. A new edit clears redo. Resuming time, loading or switching parks clears this session-only history so an old snapshot cannot rewind a running park or replace a different park. The track draft editor keeps its own undo/redo controls while active. Save files contain the current park state, never the undo history.

From **New Park**, choose Sandbox and click **Map** to cycle 32, 64, 128 and 256 tiles per side. The 32-tile choice retains the familiar flat starter paths, guests and coaster. Larger maps start empty with flat heights, $1,000 cash and the existing lake/shore materials. Click **Landscape** to cycle Flat or rolling hills from seeds 1–3. Challenge parks remain the fixed 32-tile scenario layout. Press Enter on New Park to create the selected park; the Create action remains the initial keyboard focus.

Larger maps start with a central 32×32 owned area and a one-tile owned corridor to the west edge. Owned and unowned terrain have different colors and the ownership boundary is marked. In **Build**, choose **Buy Land / $5**, then click an unowned tile touching your land. Buying is immediate, paid, and saved. Build tools reject unowned land with a reason. Lay a public path from the west edge, set a visitor entry on that path, and enable arrivals. Build access, queues, facilities and rides as usual. The camera allows proportionally closer zoom and wider panning on larger maps; **R** restores the whole-map view.

The **Raise**, **Lower**, **Water**, **Grass** and **Sand** tools in **Build** use a square brush. **Brush +/−** changes the radius from 0 to 2 (1×1, 3×3 or 5×5). Hover shows the affected area and the exact price before clicking. Raising or lowering costs $1 per changed tile, material painting $0.50, and water $2. The whole brush is rejected without payment if any tile is unowned, blocked, at an invalid height or unaffordable. Water needs level ground. Ground paths and objects block flooding; a bridge at height 2 or more can remain over water. New ground paths on water are rejected. The **T** terrain preset shortcut is limited to a fresh, empty, fully owned park; choose the landscape in New Park for large maps.

To build a tunnel, switch to **Underground view**, select **Path** or **Queue**, and set the path height so its entire clearance fits below the ground. A one-step slope needs three height steps of space. The build hint says **Tunnel** on a valid buried tile. Paths crossing through the ground remain invalid. Buried paths and the guests walking on them are hidden in Surface view and selectable in Underground view. Lowering the terrain exposes a buried path without changing its identity or connectivity. Water still requires a bridge.

The supported save now holds up to 2,048 paths, 6,144 objects, 1,024 scenery objects and 512 guest records in a 16 MiB file. Ordinary visitor arrivals stop at 256 active guests. The 32×32 map remains a quick fixture; use a larger map for a park that needs these capacities. These are correctness bounds, not performance claims for this PC.

**Decorating a path:** In Build, select a public path and choose **Bench on path / $1**. The bench appears beside the path and helps walking guests recover energy while they pass it. Select the same path and use the button again to remove the bench for a $0.50 refund. Demolishing the path also removes and refunds its bench. Existing free bins still work independently on the same path. A park may have up to 256 benches.

**Signs and fences:** With a public path selected, **Sign / $2** adds a blue information sign and **Fence / $3** adds a short wooden rail. Each can share a path with a bench and bin. Click its button again to remove it for half the paid cost ($1 or $1.50). Demolishing the path refunds all attached paid furniture. Signs and fences follow raised path height and hide with a buried path in Surface view. Each type is limited to 256 installations. Their current effect is visual wayfinding and edge detail; guests still use the path network as before.

**Paid scenery:** Choose **Tree** ($2.50) or **Flowers** ($1.50), then point to a clear owned grass or sand tile. The pointer shows the exact price or the reason placement is blocked. Demolishing paid scenery refunds half its original price ($1.25 or $0.75). Scenery already present in an older save retains its free basis and has no demolition refund. Finance lists decoration charges and refunds, including older receipts after its visible history fills; installed paid decorations count in recorded park value. Failed or unaffordable placement spends nothing and allocates no object.

For coaster engineering, close and empty the installed coaster, open **Rides [Y] → Test**, and run one isolated lap. The report lists the three fastest track pieces by piece number, kind, tile and speed. It uses the same motion rules as the live train but is a derived estimate. Changing the track or operating settings invalidates the old result. The test leaves guests, the park clock, finances and save bytes unchanged. The Track editor includes **Bank Left**, **Bank Right**, **Launch** and **Camelback / $35** pieces. A camelback starts and ends at the same height but crests one terrain step above them inside the tile. It needs three steps of vertical clearance, slows the train on ascent and accelerates it on descent. The test report counts camelbacks and includes their internal ascent, descent and maximum height. It does not simulate G forces or certify airtime. These pieces follow the existing connection, collision, quote, payment and save rules.

Guest clothing now has 48 deterministic appearances, including occasional caps. This art is generated from guest IDs and adds no save fields. The [visual bible](VISUAL_BIBLE.md) and bundled `assets/manifest.json` record the original art rules and shipped asset families.

The full Phase 3–5 roadmap gates remain open. The remaining work includes consistent construction refunds and undo for every build action, a complete large-park construction playthrough, native display and rotation checks, more original ride art and night/weather, inversions and special track, multiple independent coasters and trains, block dispatch, breakdown/repair and the complete custom-build-to-export acceptance flow. The 256-tile choice is supported by save and edit tests, but this development PC is not a performance benchmark.
